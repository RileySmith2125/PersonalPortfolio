The Hot Zone recounts stories of the first human encounters with Ebola, and dives deep into the first moment that Ebola appeared in the US- when an infected shipment of monkeys appeared in Washington D.C. and began dropping dead. It follows the military biohazard team that got called in to handle this situation, detailing the team's rapid and covert response to a highly lethal and barely understood virus.

My thoughts:
This book had me gripped from start to finish, minus Chapter 4 which was an unnecessary epilogue and a waste of time.

The early human encounters with Ebola are all fascinating and each is a thriller in its own right, which the author definitely capitalizes on.

That being said, I think the author takes this too far. The book is clearly first and foremost a story, a thriller story, and not educational. That’s not to say I didn’t learn a lot, but learning came second.

That on its own is fine- what bothered me by the end of the book though was the level of sensationalism (and to a certain extent, misinformation) about Ebola. Even with the little knowledge I had going in, I felt I had to take many of the author’s claims with a grain of salt because they seemed so outlandish. I couldn’t figure out for a while if people “liquefying” from Ebola was just a figure of speech or literal… it was said enough times by the end of the book that I believe it was literal, and this unfortunately (or fortunately) is not true. Ebola has never liquefied anyone (or their insides). Not to mention the constant anthropomorphizing of the virus as well as theorizing about potential world-ending scenarios.

Because of this I do wonder if this book was bad for the world; given that it was published in 1994 and a massive bestseller, it no doubt has a lasting impact on public perception of Ebola. I can’t gripe too much though, because for as much as the sensationalism got old, the book was, morbidly, as gruesome as you would hope a book about Ebola would be.
